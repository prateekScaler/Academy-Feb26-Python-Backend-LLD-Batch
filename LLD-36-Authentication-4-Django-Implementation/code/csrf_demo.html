What evil.com's page contains

<form action="https://yourbank.com/transfer" method="POST" id="f">
  <input name="to" value="attacker"><input name="amount" value="5000">
</form>
<script>document.getElementById('f').submit()</script>

Notice evil.com never learns your cookie — it doesn't need to. It just points a request at the bank and the browser attaches your cookie for it. That's the whole trick.

Takeaways

  • SameSite is the modern fix — it decides whether the cookie is even attached to a cross-site request.
  • CSRF tokens work at a different layer: the cookie is sent, but the request lacks a secret only your own pages know. Still needed when you must run SameSite=None.
  • Bearer headers are immune by construction — the browser never auto-attaches an Authorization header, so there's nothing for evil.com to ride on.
